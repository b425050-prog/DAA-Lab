Worst-case analysis requested by the problem
--------------------------------------------
Method 1 sequential: merge lengths 2n, 3n, ..., kn.
  Worst-case comparisons = n * (k(k+1)/2 - 1) - (k - 1) = 133 for this n,k.
  Output writes across merges = n * (k(k+1)/2 - 1) = 140 for this n,k.
  Therefore the running time is Theta(n k^2).

Method 2 balanced: at most ceil(log2 k) = 3 merge rounds.
  At each round, at most nk = 32 elements participate in merging.
  Hence work is at most proportional to nk * ceil(log2 k) = 96 element-level units.
  Therefore the running time is Theta(n k log k).
  Because k is a power of two, exact worst-case key comparisons are nk log2(k) - (k-1) = 89.

Conclusion
----------
Both methods are correct, but balanced pairwise merging avoids repeatedly reprocessing one ever-growing accumulator.
For large k, Theta(n k log k) is asymptotically better than Theta(n k^2).
